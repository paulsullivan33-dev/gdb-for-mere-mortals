The lab — one machine, seven small programs, no one else's code
====================================================================

The lab is GDB as Ubuntu ships it, run on
the build machine (an Ubuntu 24.04
sandbox, 2 CPUs, 7.7 GB RAM):

  * GDB 15.1 from Ubuntu's own packages
    (the gdb package plus libbabeltrace1,
    libipt2, libsource-highlight4t64 and
    libdebuginfod1t64), downloaded and
    unpacked — not installed — into a
    private folder, because the sandbox
    does not permit system-wide installs.
    A small wrapper exports
    LD_LIBRARY_PATH pointing at the
    unpacked libraries and runs the real
    binary, so transcripts show plain
    `gdb` throughout.
  * GCC 13.3.0, already installed on the
    machine, compiled every fixture.
  * Seven fixture programs written for
    the guide (gdb-lab/fixtures/):
    sum.c      two numbers added in a
               helper; the first session.
    store.c    a three-slot store asked
               for a fourth slot; the
               segfault of chapter 5.
    basket.c   five shop items and an
               off-by-one loop; chapters
               6 and 7.
    convert.c  Celsius to Fahrenheit with
               an integer-division bug;
               chapter 9. Right at 0,
               wrong at 100 and -40.
    countdown.c  recursion, for the stack
               of chapter 8.
    shrunk.c   built twice, -O0 and -O2,
               for chapter 14.
    sleeper.c  prints a tick a second;
               the lab's own process for
               the attach attempt.
  * Interactive transcripts were recorded
    through a terminal session so the
    (gdb) prompts and the typed commands
    are the session as it happened; batch
    and shell transcripts are the commands
    as run, in order, complete.

What the lab ran, in
gdb-lab/transcripts/:

  * t00 versions: gcc 13.3.0, gdb 15.1.
  * t01 first session on sum: break,
    run, step in, prints, finish (with
    the not-yet-assigned `total` printing
    garbage until one `next` completed
    the line — kept in the transcript and
    explained in chapter 4), continue.
  * t02 the crash: SIGSEGV at store.c:23,
    backtrace of two frames, slot = 0x0.
  * t03 breakpoints: by function, by
    line with `if i == 2`, the ledger,
    a `next` over the loop's edge.
  * t04 the watchpoint: `watch total`,
    five firings (the initialisation
    plus four additions) for five items,
    i = 3 and items[4] read at the last
    firing, the watchpoint deleted
    before the clean exit.
  * t05 convert plainly at 100, 0, -40,
    then one batch session: print (9/5)
    is 1, print fahr is 132.
  * t06 frames: four-deep backtrace in
    countdown, frame 2 visited, its n
    read as 3.
  * t07 cores: the pattern is `core`;
    `ulimit -c unlimited` is refused
    (the sandbox's hard limit is 0 and
    cannot be raised from inside), the
    crash leaves no file; gcore writes
    basket.core at a chosen stop, and a
    fresh session reads i = 2 and
    total = 409 back out of it.
  * t08 the lab's .gdbinit, in its own
    home (gdb-lab/gdbinit): pagination
    off, listsize 20, `bmain` defined
    and used, a struct pretty-printed,
    the history file in place.
  * t09 shrunk both ways: -O0 prints 21
    and 43; -O2 stops on a moved line
    and reports argc, argv, seed and
    answer all <optimized out>.
  * t11 the same crash compiled with
    -fsanitize=address: its report names
    the write, the zero page, and the
    same two frames.
  * t90 the dark: store built without
    -g (print refused, info locals
    empty) and stripped (break main
    refused by name; the crash stops at
    a bare address).

What the lab could not do:

  * Attach. The kernel's ptrace scope
    is 1 in the sandbox, so gdb may
    attach only to its own descendants;
    the lab's sleeper is a sibling, and
    gdb refused, naming the setting
    (t10). Chapter 12 teaches attach
    around that verified refusal; a
    successful attach is documented
    only.
  * A kernel-written core file, for the
    reason in t07. Chapter 10's
    examination half is verified against
    the gcore file instead.
  * Graphical front-ends, remote
    debugging, other languages' runtimes
    and multithreaded fixtures are
    characterised in the text, not run;
    criteria.md keeps the full ledger.

Nothing in the lab is modelled on any
real person, program or organisation.
The data is a shopping list, three
temperatures and numbers small enough
to check by hand; the only processes
touched were the lab's own.
